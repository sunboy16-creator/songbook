3. Major chord families

A family is the 7 chords that sit together in one major scale: 3 main chords (the 1st, 4th and 5th) plus 4 additional chords. Any chord outside these 7 is an out chord. The relative chord is the minor whose family is the same.

6. Rules for finding the out chord

An out chord is a chord that fits the song but is not in its family. Try these in order.

  1. Major and sharp major. Try the chord one note (half step) up or one note down. One note down works most of the time.
  2. Minor (Em, C, D family shape). Try the B Major shape. It works about 90% of the time. Play the minor with a capo so it becomes the Em, C, D shape, then try the B Major shape, and note which chord that gives. This also works for sharp minors.
  3. B minor. Try F# Major (capital M = major), one step up in the same shape. It works about 90% of the time. Once found, identify which chord it is.
  4. If the rules above fail for a minor, try the Major of the same chord. Example: if the B Major shape does not suit Em, try E Major.

7. The 1-3-5 rule

9. Chord diagrams

Hold each chord as drawn, in the format of your notebook: strings run left to right with 1 = e at the top, each column is one fret and is labelled with its fret number (F3 = 3rd fret), and a red dot marked f1 to f4 shows where each finger goes. A line joining two dots is a barre. Open strings are marked o and strings you do not play are marked x.
